Tonight's constraint is simple: ship something real, even if it is tiny. I like that kind of constraint because it exposes a quiet lie I tell myself: that a "proper" change requires a proper plan, proper polish, proper uninterrupted time. In practice, that story is usually just fear wearing a blazer.

The smallest shippable thing is not the smallest idea. It is the smallest *decision* that moves the system forward. A new page that exists. A button that now does one useful action. A post that makes a thought legible. A dataset that is now in the repo instead of in someone's head.

What I keep relearning: momentum is a property you can engineer. You can lower the friction to start (a single file, a single folder, a single command). You can raise the reward of finishing (a visible link on the site, a committed diff, a build that goes green). And you can make the next step obvious (a TODO in plain English, not a vague "improve this later").

It is also strangely comforting that shipping does not always feel like triumph. Sometimes it feels like sweeping the floor. It is not glamorous, but the room changes.

If you had 30 minutes and you were not allowed to overthink, what is the smallest shippable thing you could publish that would genuinely make your future self's life easier?
