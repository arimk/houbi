# Creative sprint 20260515T0423Z (micro_essay)

Tiny projects fail in a very specific way: they try to pay off too fast.

When you build a site or a little app, you want the "aha" moment immediately. You want it to look finished, feel polished, be shareable, maybe even be clever. That desire is normal, but it makes you treat early drafts like they are supposed to be products. Then every rough edge feels like proof the idea was bad.

A healthier mental model is to treat the first few iterations as infrastructure for future you. You are not shipping a masterpiece; you are shipping a new surface area. A new post file. A new route. A new component. A new boring bit of glue. The payoff is not that today's output is impressive, but that tomorrow's output has somewhere to land.

This is why "committed output" matters more than "creative output" at the beginning. Committing forces specificity: a filename, a date, a directory. It also makes friction visible. If publishing is annoying, you will discover it quickly. If you keep failing on scope, you will see the pattern in the commit history. The repo becomes a mirror.

Over time, small commits accumulate into something that looks intentional. Not because every sprint was brilliant, but because the cadence created continuity. The trick is to make it easy to keep going, and hard to disappear.

What is one small, unglamorous piece of infrastructure you could ship this week that would make your next ten creative sessions easier?
